Methodology & Formula

Biodiesel (FAME — Fatty Acid Methyl Ester) blends with petroleum diesel by simple volume mixing. Cetane, density, and viscosity follow linear volume-weighted blending, making blend properties predictable from the two component fuels.

Blended Cetane

Cetaneblend = Cetanediesel × (1 − x) + Cetanebio × x

Blended Density

ρblend = ρdiesel × (1 − x) + ρbio × x

Blended Viscosity

νblend = νdiesel × (1 − x) + νbio × x

  • x = Biodiesel volume fraction (e.g., 0.20 for B20)
  • Biodiesel cetane (50–65) exceeds diesel (40–50), improving ignition quality and reducing cold-start noise
  • Higher density slightly increases energy per litre but injector calibration is volume-based, so net energy per injection drops ~1–2% per 10% blend
  • Higher viscosity improves lubricity (a benefit for low-sulfar diesel) but increases as temperature falls

Cold-Flow Behavior (CFPP)

Biodiesel gels at higher temperatures than petroleum diesel — B100 CFPP is typically 10–15°C warmer than the base diesel. Each 10% of biodiesel raises the blend's cloud point and Cold Filter Plugging Point (CFPP). For winter operation above B5, verify CFPP against your minimum ambient temperature or use cold-flow additives / heated storage.

Reference Standard: ASTM D6751 / EN 14214

ASTM D6751 (US) and EN 14214 (Europe) define the specifications for neat biodiesel (B100) used in blending — including cetane number, density, viscosity, and cold-flow limits. Blends are then governed by the diesel fuel standard (ASTM D975 / EN 590), which permits up to B7 in standard EN 590 diesel and B20 under ASTM D7467.
