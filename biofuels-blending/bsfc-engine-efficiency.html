Methodology & Formula

BSFC expresses how much fuel an engine burns to produce one unit of power for one hour — the universal measure of engine fuel efficiency. From BSFC and the fuel's energy content, we derive the thermal efficiency: the fraction of fuel energy actually converted into useful work.

Brake Specific Fuel Consumption

BSFC = Fuel Flow (g/hr) / Power (kW)  [g/kWh]

Thermal Efficiency

η = 3600 / (BSFC × LHV) × 100%

  • 3600 = 3.6 MJ/kWh expressed in kJ, since BSFC×LHV yields kJ of fuel energy per kWh of output
  • LHV = Lower Heating Value — the standard basis for engine efficiency, excluding latent heat in exhaust water vapor
  • The efficiency figure is brake thermal efficiency — based on power actually delivered at the crankshaft, after friction and pumping losses
  • For ethanol or methanol, use the fuel's own (lower) LHV — efficiency numbers are only comparable when the correct LHV is used

Reading BSFC Maps

BSFC varies across the engine's speed/load range — best efficiency usually sits at moderate-to-high load, mid RPM. A single-point BSFC (like this tool computes) is useful for a specific operating condition; full engine development uses a BSFC map across the whole operating envelope to find the efficiency "island".

Reference Standard: SAE J1349 (Engine Power Test Code)

SAE J1349 is the standard test code for measuring engine power and fuel consumption in automotive applications, defining correction factors and measurement procedures. BSFC and brake thermal efficiency derived per this standard are the basis for engine specifications, emissions certification, and fuel-economy development.
