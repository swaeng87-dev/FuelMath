Methodology & Formula

Octane ratings measure a fuel's resistance to engine knock, but different regions use different scales. Europe, Asia, and Australia display RON at the pump; the US and Canada display the AKI — the average of RON and MON — which is why a US "87 octane" equals a European "91".

AKI (Anti-Knock Index) — US Pump Rating

AKI = (RON + MON) / 2

Octane Sensitivity

S = RON − MON

Reverse — Recover RON & MON from AKI

RON = AKI + S/2  •  MON = AKI − S/2

  • RON (Research Octane Number) — measured under mild, low-speed conditions (ASTM D2699); typically the higher number
  • MON (Motor Octane Number) — measured under harsher, higher-load conditions (ASTM D2700); typically 8–12 points below RON
  • AKI = the average of the two, shown on North American pumps as (R+M)/2
  • The reverse direction needs an assumed sensitivity (default 9) since AKI alone can't uniquely determine RON and MON

Why the Confusion Matters

A driver comparing a US "91 premium" (AKI) with a European "95" (RON) is comparing different scales — the US 91 AKI is roughly a 95 RON. Importing vehicles, tuning engines, or blending fuels across markets requires converting to a common basis first, which is exactly what this tool does.

Reference Standard: ASTM D2699 / D2700

ASTM D2699 defines the Research Octane Number test method and ASTM D2700 defines the Motor Octane Number, both using standardized cooperative test engines. The AKI — (R+M)/2 — is the labeling convention mandated for retail gasoline dispensers in the United States by the EPA and FTC.
