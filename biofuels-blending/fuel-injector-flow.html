Methodology & Formula

Injector sizing starts with the engine's total fuel demand — set by horsepower and BSFC — then divides that demand across the cylinders while respecting a safe maximum duty cycle so the injectors never run at 100% (where they lose control and can overheat).

Total Engine Fuel Demand

Fueltotal = HP × BSFC  [lb/hr]

Flow per Injector (with duty cycle)

Flowlb/hr = Fueltotal / (Cylinders × Duty)

Convert to Volumetric Flow

Flowcc/min = (Flowlb/hr × 453.6) / (Density × 60)

  • BSFC = Brake Specific Fuel Consumption (lb of fuel per hp per hour) — the engine's fuel efficiency at power
  • Duty cycle = fraction of time the injector is open at peak RPM; 0.80 (80%) is the common safe maximum
  • 453.6 = grams per pound, converting mass flow from lb/hr to g/hr
  • Density converts mass flow (g/hr) to volume flow (cc/min); higher-density fuels like E85 need larger injectors for the same energy

Why Duty Cycle Matters

An injector at 100% duty is effectively "stuck open" and can no longer meter fuel precisely — mixture control is lost exactly when the engine needs it most. Sizing to ≤80% duty preserves accurate control, keeps injector temperatures down, and leaves margin for fuel-pressure variation and future power increases.

Reference: Internal Combustion Engine Fuel Delivery Sizing

This method reflects standard engine-building and EFI tuning practice for selecting fuel injectors. Injector flow ratings are published at a reference pressure (commonly 3 bar / 43.5 psi); actual delivered flow scales with the square root of pressure ratio, so confirm the rating pressure matches your system before finalizing a part number.
