GCV vs NCV — Quick Guide

GCV (HHV): All combustion products cooled to 25°C — includes latent heat recovered from condensing water vapor.

NCV (LHV): Water leaves as vapor — latent heat is lost up the stack. This is what real boilers and engines actually deliver.

Methodology & Formula

The difference between Gross Calorific Value (GCV) and Net Calorific Value (NCV) is the latent heat of vaporization of water formed during combustion. When hydrogen burns, it produces water vapor (2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O); if that vapor escapes up the stack without condensing, its latent heat is lost.

NCV = GCV − (9 × H% / 100 + M% / 100) × L
  • H% = Hydrogen content by mass (%) — each kg of H produces 9 kg of water (hence the factor 9)
  • M% = Moisture content by mass (%) — inherent fuel moisture also absorbs latent heat
  • L = Latent heat of steam at 25°C = 587 kcal/kg = 2.456 MJ/kg
  • For hydrogen-rich fuels (natural gas: 25% H), the GCV–NCV gap is ~10%; for coal (~5% H), only ~3%

Commercial Caution

Fuel contracts may specify GCV or NCV as the billing basis. Comparing a GCV-based quote against an NCV-based quote without conversion creates a systematic 3–10% pricing error. Always confirm the calorific basis before comparing fuel tenders.

Reference Standard: ASTM D240 / ISO 1928

ASTM D240 covers heat of combustion of liquid hydrocarbon fuels by bomb calorimeter; ISO 1928 covers solid mineral fuels. Both standards define the GCV measurement procedure and provide the hydrogen-correction method for deriving NCV used in commercial energy accounting.
