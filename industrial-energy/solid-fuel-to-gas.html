Methodology & Formula

Replacing solid fuels with natural gas in kilns, furnaces, and boilers requires an equivalent heat release analysis. Because gas burners typically achieve higher thermal efficiency than solid fuel combustion systems, the required gas volume is often lower than a raw energy-content ratio would suggest.

Step 1 — Useful Heat from Solid Fuel

E = msolid × GCVsolid × ηsolid

Step 2 — Equivalent Gas Volume

Vgas = E / (GCVgas × ηgas)

Step 3 — Operating Cost Differential

ΔC = (Vgas × Pgas) − (msolid × Psolid)

  • msolid = Solid fuel mass (kg), η = Thermal efficiency of each system
  • Solid fuel systems typically run at 65–78% efficiency; gas burners achieve 82–92%
  • ASTM D5865 bomb calorimetry provides the GCV basis for coal and petcoke; always use as-received (not dry) GCV values for operational comparisons

Beyond Fuel Cost

Gas conversion economics often improve further when accounting for: eliminated ash handling and disposal costs, reduced boiler maintenance, lower emissions compliance costs (SOx, NOx, particulates), and improved process temperature control. These savings are not included in this fuel-only comparison.

Reference Standard: ASTM D5865 / Equivalent Heat Release Analysis

ASTM D5865 is the standard test method for gross calorific value of coal and coke using bomb calorimetry. Equivalent heat release analysis normalizes different fuels to a common useful-energy basis before cost comparison.
