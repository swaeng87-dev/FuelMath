Methodology & Formula

The Calculated Carbon Aromaticity Index (CCAI) is an empirical indicator of marine fuel ignition quality, derived from density and viscosity. Higher CCAI values indicate greater aromatic carbon content, which delays ignition in marine diesel engines and increases the risk of incomplete combustion, soot formation, and thermal stress.

CCAI = ρ − 140.7 − 15.6 × log₁₀(ν)
  • ρ = Fuel density at 15°C (kg/m³)
  • ν = Kinematic viscosity at 50°C (cSt = mm²/s)
  • CCAI below 820 indicates good ignition; values above 850 require engine manufacturer consultation before bunkering
  • CIMAC recommends CCAI limits in bunker delivery notes; many engine OEMs specify maximum CCAI in warranty terms

Operational Guidance

Fuels with CCAI > 850 may require: adjusted injection timing, higher compression pressure, pre-heating optimization, or blending with lower-CCAI components. Always cross-check against the engine maker's fuel approval list before acceptance.

Reference Standard: ISO 8217 / CIMAC Guidelines

ISO 8217 defines the specification for marine distillate (DMX, DMA, DMZ, DMB) and residual (RMA–RMK) fuel grades. The CCAI calculation method is published in ISO 8217 Annex D and endorsed by CIMAC (International Council on Large Engines) for ignition quality assessment where direct cetane measurement is impractical.
