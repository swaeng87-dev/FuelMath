Why Pre-Heat?

Heavy fuel oils at storage temperature (380–700 cSt) cannot atomize properly. Pre-heating reduces viscosity to the 12–15 cSt window, ensuring fine droplet formation, complete combustion, and minimal soot on burner components.

Methodology & Formula

The ASTM D341 viscosity-temperature relationship uses a double-logarithmic model that accurately tracks how petroleum viscosity falls with rising temperature. Given two measured reference points, the model constants A and B are solved, then the equation is inverted to find the temperature producing the target atomization viscosity.

ASTM D341 Core Equation

log log(ν + 0.7) = A − B × log(T)

Step 1 — Solve Model Constant B

B = [W(ν₁) − W(ν₂)] / [log(T₂) − log(T₁)]

Step 2 — Solve Model Constant A

A = W(ν₁) + B × log(T₁)

Step 3 — Invert for Pre-Heat Temperature

T = 10[(A − W(νtarget)) / B]

  • W(ν) = log log(ν + 0.7) — the ASTM D341 transformation function
  • T = Absolute temperature in Kelvin (°C + 273.15)
  • ν₁, ν₂ = Two measured viscosities (cSt) at temperatures T₁, T₂
  • The +0.7 offset is the ASTM D341 empirical constant for liquid petroleum products

Reference Standard: ASTM D341

ASTM D341 defines the standard viscosity-temperature charts and equations for liquid petroleum products. The double-logarithmic relationship is accurate across the normal operating range of fuel oils (typically 30°C to 150°C). For temperatures outside this range or non-petroleum fluids, direct laboratory measurement is recommended.
