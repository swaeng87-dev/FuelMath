Methodology & Formula

Crude oil is traded by volume (barrels) but shipped and refined by mass (metric tons). Because density varies significantly between light and heavy grades, the barrels-to-tons conversion factor is not constant — it depends on the specific crude's API gravity.

Step 1 — Specific Gravity from API

SG = 141.5 / (°API + 131.5)

Step 2 — Density at 15°C

ρ = SG × 999 kg/m³

Step 3 — Mass Conversion

MT = bbl × 158.987 L/bbl × ρ / 1,000,000

  • 1 barrel (bbl) = 158.987 litres = 0.158987 m³ = 42 US gallons
  • 999 kg/m³ = Density of water at 15°C (API reference)
  • Typical industry rule of thumb: light crude ≈ 7.3–7.5 bbl/ton; heavy crude ≈ 6.0–6.5 bbl/ton
  • Higher °API = lighter crude = more barrels per metric ton

Reference Standard: OPEC / BP Statistical Energy Conversion Standards

OPEC's Annual Statistical Bulletin and BP's Statistical Review of World Energy publish the standard barrel-to-ton conversion factors used in global energy statistics. The worldwide average is approximately 7.33 bbl/ton (OPEC basket basis), but contract-specific conversions must use the actual grade's API gravity for accuracy.
