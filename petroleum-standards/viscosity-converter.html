Methodology & Formula

Viscosity is reported in two fundamentally different forms: kinematic viscosity (ν) — the fluid's resistance to flow under gravity — and dynamic viscosity (μ) — its internal resistance to shear. The two are related by fluid density, making density an essential input for accurate conversion.

Core Relationship

μ (cP) = ν (cSt) × ρ (g/cm³)

SI Conversions

ν (m²/s) = ν (cSt) × 10⁻⁶

μ (Pa·s) = μ (cP) × 10⁻³

Saybolt Universal Seconds (SSU) Empirical Conversion

SSU > 100: ν (cSt) = 0.220 × SSU − 135/SSU

SSU ≤ 100: ν (cSt) = 0.226 × SSU − 195/SSU

  • All inputs are first normalized to kinematic cSt, then converted to every target unit — ensuring consistent results regardless of input format
  • ASTM D445 specifies kinematic viscosity measurement by capillary viscometer at controlled temperatures (40°C and 100°C for lubricants, 50°C for fuel oils)
  • SSU (Saybolt) is a legacy US unit still encountered in older lubricant specifications and some marine fuel reports

Reference Standard: ASTM D445 / ISO 3104

ASTM D445 and ISO 3104 define the standard test method for kinematic viscosity of transparent and opaque liquids. The SSU conversion formulas are derived from ASTM D2161, which provides the standard tables for converting Saybolt viscosities to kinematic units.
