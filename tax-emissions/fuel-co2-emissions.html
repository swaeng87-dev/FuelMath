Methodology & Formula

Every fossil fuel contains carbon. When burned, that carbon combines with atmospheric oxygen to form CO₂ — so emissions are directly proportional to the fuel consumed, via a fuel-specific emission factor.

CO₂ = Quantity × Emission Factor
  • Quantity = fuel consumed (litres, SCM, or kg depending on fuel)
  • Emission factor = kg CO₂ per unit of fuel — set by the fuel's carbon content and combustion chemistry
  • t CO₂ = kg ÷ 1000, the standard unit for corporate reporting
  • Diesel emits ~15% more CO₂ per litre than petrol due to higher carbon content and density

Why CO₂ Exceeds the Fuel's Weight

It seems surprising that 1 L of diesel (~0.84 kg) produces 2.68 kg of CO₂ — but the extra mass is oxygen pulled from the air during combustion. Each carbon atom (12 u) binds two oxygen atoms (2×16 u), so the CO₂ molecule is ~3.7× the mass of the carbon alone. This is why fuel emissions are greater than the fuel's own weight.

Reference Standard: IPCC Tier 1 / EPA Emission Factors

The IPCC Tier 1 method applies default emission factors to activity data (fuel consumed) — the standard first-tier approach for national inventories and corporate Scope 1 reporting. These direct combustion emissions fall under Scope 1 of the GHG Protocol. For a full footprint, add CH₄ and N₂O (using GWPs) and upstream Scope 3 emissions.
