Methodology & Formula

Unburned methane released to the atmosphere is both an environmental liability (potent greenhouse gas) and an economic loss (lost product). The Global Warming Potential (GWP) converts a methane mass into the equivalent mass of CO₂ over a chosen time horizon.

CO₂-Equivalent

CO₂e = CH₄ mass × GWP

GWP Multipliers (IPCC AR6)

GWP100 = 29.8  •  GWP20 = 82.5

Economic Loss (optional)

Loss = CH₄ mass × Gas price

  • GWP100 = 29.8 — the standard reporting horizon used in national inventories and most corporate disclosures
  • GWP20 = 82.5 — captures methane's intense short-term warming; increasingly used to prioritize near-term abatement
  • Methane persists ~12 years in the atmosphere before breaking down, hence the higher short-term multiplier
  • 1 kg CH₄ ≈ 1.4 SCM — use the SCM-to-kg converter to translate volume measurements to mass

Why Methane Leaks Undermine Gas as a "Bridge Fuel"

Natural gas only has a climate advantage over coal if leakage stays low. Because methane is so potent short-term, a leak rate above ~3% can erase gas's CO₂ benefit versus coal for power generation. This is why leak detection and repair (LDAR) programs are central to methane-reduction pledges.

Reference Standard: IPCC AR6 / EPA Methane Challenge

The GWP100 = 29.8 and GWP20 = 82.5 values are from the IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (AR6). The EPA Methane Challenge and the Global Methane Pledge (target: cut methane 30% by 2030) drive corporate and national methane accounting, for which this CO₂e conversion is the foundational step.
