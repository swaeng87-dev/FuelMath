Methodology & Formula

Gas is bought and sold for its energy content, not just its volume. When the delivered calorific value differs from the contractual basis, the unit price is adjusted proportionally so the buyer pays for the energy actually received and the seller is compensated fairly for higher-quality gas.

Adjusted Unit Price

Padj = Pcontract × (GCVdelivered / GCVcontract)

Total Adjustment Amount

Δ = (Padj − Pcontract) × Volume

  • Positive Δ = delivered GCV exceeded contract → buyer pays more → credit to seller
  • Negative Δ = delivered GCV fell short → buyer pays less → penalty to seller
  • The adjustment is linear in the GCV ratio — a 2% GCV shortfall produces a 2% price reduction
  • Contract and delivered GCV must use the same basis (both GCV or both NCV) for the ratio to be valid

Beyond Simple Price Adjustment

Real GSAs often add quality tolerances, off-spec penalties, and Wobbe Index limits on top of the linear GCV adjustment. Gas outside tolerance may be rejected outright or subject to additional liquidated damages. This tool models the core proportional price adjustment — check your specific contract for tolerance bands and extra penalty clauses.

Reference: Gas Sales Agreement (GSA) Quality Adjustment Formula

Proportional calorific-value price adjustment is the standard mechanism in pipeline gas and LNG sales contracts worldwide. It ensures energy-equivalent pricing across supply periods where gas composition (and therefore heating value) naturally varies, protecting both buyer and seller.
