Methodology & Formula

Under-inflated tires deform more with each rotation, increasing rolling resistance. The engine must work harder to maintain speed, burning extra fuel. The US Department of Energy quantifies this loss at approximately 0.2% of fuel economy for every 1 psi of under-inflation.

Step 1 — Fuel Economy Penalty

Penalty% = Under-inflation (psi) × 0.2

Step 2 — Baseline Fuel Requirement

Vbase = Distance / Mileage

Step 3 — Extra Fuel & Cost

Vextra = Vbase × (Penalty% / 100)  •  Cost = Vextra × Price

  • 0.2% per psi = US DOE coefficient for combined under-inflation of all four tires
  • Vbase = Fuel the trip would use at correct pressure
  • Vextra = Additional fuel wasted due to tire drag
  • The penalty is linear in this model — real-world loss can be slightly higher at severe under-inflation

Beyond Fuel: Safety & Tire Life

Under-inflation also causes uneven/accelerated tread wear (up to 25% shorter tire life), poor handling, longer braking distances, and increased blowout risk — especially under load at highway speeds. The fuel saving alone rarely captures the full value of correct tire pressure.

Reference Standard: US DOE Fuel Economy & Tire Pressure Loss Coefficient

The US Department of Energy and National Highway Traffic Safety Administration publish the 0.2% per psi fuel economy loss figure used in federal fuel-saving guidance. It is the standard coefficient for fleet tire-pressure management programs and eco-driving education.
