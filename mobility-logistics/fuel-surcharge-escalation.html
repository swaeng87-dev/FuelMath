Methodology & Formula

A Fuel Surcharge (FSC) decouples the volatile fuel component from the base freight rate, so contracts stay fair as diesel prices move. The surcharge applies the percentage change in fuel price — scaled by an agreed pass-through — on top of the base rate.

Step 1 — Fuel Price Change

Δ = (Current − Baseline) / Baseline

Step 2 — Fuel Surcharge Percentage

FSC% = Δ × Pass-Through%

Step 3 — Adjusted Freight Rate

New Rate = Base Rate × (1 + FSC)

  • Baseline price = the fuel price the contract rate was originally priced against
  • Pass-through = the share of fuel price change transferred to the freight rate (100% = full transfer)
  • Symmetric — positive Δ raises the rate; negative Δ lowers it
  • More sophisticated FSC models account for fuel's share of total operating cost (often 30–40% for road freight), rather than applying the full fuel-price change

Refinement: Fuel-Share Weighting

In practice, many carriers apply the FSC only to the fuel portion of their cost base. If fuel is 35% of operating cost, a 20% fuel rise justifies roughly a 7% freight increase (0.35 × 20%), not 20%. Use the pass-through field to model either approach — set it to the fuel-share × 100 for cost-weighted escalation.

Reference Standard: Standard FSC (Fuel Surcharge) Index Formula Model

The FSC index formula is the industry-standard mechanism for freight contract escalation, published by transport associations and used in shipper–carrier agreements worldwide. It references a published fuel price index (national average diesel) at defined review intervals — weekly, monthly, or per contract period.
