Methodology & Formula

Driver behavior is the largest controllable variable in fleet fuel economy — aggressive acceleration, poor gear selection, excessive speed, and idling can degrade mileage by 15–30%. This tool grades each driver against a route-class benchmark and prices the cost of their underperformance.

Step 1 — Efficiency Ratio

Ratio = (Actual Mileage / Target Mileage) × 100%

Step 2 — Excess Fuel Consumed

Vexcess = (Distance / Actual) − (Distance / Target)

Step 3 — Underperformance Cost & Grade

Penalty = Vexcess × Price  →  Grade (A/B/C/D)

  • Target Mileage = Fleet benchmark for the route class (same vehicle, payload, and terrain profile)
  • Actual Mileage = Driver's measured fuel economy from trip data
  • Ratio ≥ 100% = Grade A (at or above target); < 80% = Grade D (training/coaching required)
  • Benchmarks must be route-class specific — comparing a hilly heavy-haul route against a flat highway run produces false grades

Key Driver Behavior Factors

Telematics data links fuel waste to: harsh acceleration & braking events, sustained over-speed (>90 km/h for trucks), excessive idling, improper gear selection, and failure to use engine braking. Grade D drivers typically show multiple of these patterns and benefit most from eco-driving coaching.

Reference Standard: Commercial Fleet Telematics Benchmark Index

Fleet telematics platforms benchmark driver fuel performance against route-class baselines using GPS, fuel-level sensors, and CAN-bus data. The A–D grading thresholds used here reflect common fleet incentive program structures, where top-quartile drivers receive fuel bonuses and bottom-quartile drivers enter coaching programs.
