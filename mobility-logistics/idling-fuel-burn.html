Methodology & Formula

Idling burns fuel while producing zero distance — it is pure waste. Whether from traffic congestion, loading operations, cab climate control, or power take-off (PTO) equipment, idling converts fuel directly into cost and emissions with no productive output.

Daily Fuel Wasted

Vday = Idling Hours × Burn Rate

Monthly Fuel Wasted

Vmonth = Vday × Operating Days

Monthly Financial Loss

Costmonth = Vmonth × Fuel Price

  • Idling Hours = Total idling time per day per vehicle
  • Burn Rate = Fuel consumption while stationary (L/hr) — varies by engine size, load, and accessories
  • Operating Days = Working days per month (default 26)
  • Annualize by multiplying monthly loss × 12 for full-year budgeting

Beyond Fuel Cost

Idling also accelerates engine wear (incomplete combustion, oil dilution), increases maintenance intervals, and produces localized emissions. These hidden costs typically add 20–40% on top of the direct fuel figure. Anti-idling regulations in many cities also carry fines.

Reference Standard: EPA / Fleet Idling Consumption Guidelines

The US EPA and Argonne National Laboratory publish idling fuel consumption data: heavy-duty diesel trucks idle at approximately 0.6–1.0 gallons per hour (2.3–3.8 L/hr). These figures form the basis of fleet idle-reduction programs and anti-idling policy cost-benefit analyses.
