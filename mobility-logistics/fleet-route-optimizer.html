Methodology & Formula

Inter-state long-haul routes cross multiple tax jurisdictions, creating fuel price differentials of 5–15% between regions. Fleet operators who strategically refuel at the lowest-priced stops on their route compound significant savings across thousands of trips per year.

Step 1 — Collect Candidate Prices

P = {p₁, p₂, p₃, p₄} (excluding blank stops)

Step 2 — Identify Minimum

p* = min(P) → recommended stop index

Step 3 — Optimized Total Cost

Costoptimal = Fuel Needed × p*

  • Fuel Needed = Total litres required for the trip (distance ÷ loaded mileage)
  • p₁–p₄ = Pump prices at each candidate stop along the route
  • Blank price fields are automatically excluded from the comparison
  • For a 15% price differential on a 350 L fill, the saving between worst and best stop is substantial — multiply across a fleet's annual mileage for the full impact

Operational Constraints

This optimizer identifies the cheapest price point but real routing must also respect: tank capacity limits (can you reach the cheap stop with current fuel?), minimum reserve requirements, depot access hours, and driver duty-time regulations. For multi-leg journeys exceeding tank range, plan partial fills — buy enough at each stop to reach the next cheapest point.

Fuel routing optimization is a standard practice in fleet logistics, where regional tax differentials create arbitrage opportunities. Large fleet operators integrate live fuel price APIs into route planning systems; this tool provides the core decision logic for single-trip manual planning.
