Methodology & Formula

The air-conditioning compressor is engine-driven, so running it adds a mechanical load that the engine must overcome with extra fuel. The penalty is largest at low speeds and idle, where the compressor represents a bigger fraction of total engine output.

Step 1 — Baseline Fuel (AC Off)

Vbase = Distance / Mileage

Step 2 — Fuel with AC On

VAC = Vbase / (1 − Penalty / 100)

Step 3 — Extra Cost

Cost = (VAC − Vbase) × Price

  • Penalty = AC fuel consumption increase (%) — varies by speed, climate, and compressor duty cycle
  • Division form — because AC reduces mileage, the fuel required rises as Vbase ÷ (1 − penalty), not Vbase × (1 + penalty)
  • City driving suffers the highest penalty; steady highway cruising the lowest
  • Modern variable-displacement and electric compressors reduce the penalty versus older fixed units

AC vs. Open Windows Trade-Off

Below ~60–80 km/h, open windows add little drag — ventilation is cheaper than AC. Above that speed, window turbulence increases aerodynamic drag enough that AC becomes the more efficient choice. This crossover is why "AC at speed, vents in town" is the standard eco-driving guidance.

Reference Standard: NREL Automotive Auxiliary Load Study

The National Renewable Energy Laboratory's auxiliary-load research quantifies AC fuel penalties of 5–25% depending on ambient conditions, vehicle size, and driving cycle. These figures underpin EPA fuel-economy label adjustments and fleet AC-management guidance.
