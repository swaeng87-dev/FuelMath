Methodology & Formula

CNG dispensers draw from a three-tier storage cascade (Low → Medium → High pressure banks). The dispenser connects the vehicle to the lowest bank whose pressure exceeds the vehicle's current pressure, equalizes, then steps up to the next bank. This staged approach maximizes mass transfer without requiring compressor intervention during every fill.

Isothermal Equalization (per bank)

Peq = (Pbank × Vbank + Pveh × Vveh) / (Vbank + Vveh)

Mass Delivered (Real Gas Law)

m = (ΔP·V)veh × M / (Z × R × T)

Mass Transfer Efficiency

η = mvehicle / mwithdrawn × 100%

  • Fill sequence: Low bank fills the vehicle from Pstart until pressures equalize, then Medium bank, then High bank — the High bank alone must reach the target fill pressure
  • M = Molar mass (methane = 16.04 g/mol), Z = Compressibility (≈0.9 at 200 bar), R = 8.314 J/(mol·K)
  • Typical cascade configurations: Low ≈ 100–150 bar, Medium ≈ 150–200 bar, High ≈ 200–250 bar
  • Typical mass transfer efficiency for a well-sized cascade: 70–90%

Model Limitation

This estimator uses an isothermal ideal-gas equalization model. Real fills exhibit temperature rise (Joule-Thomson and compression heating), which reduces mass density and final state-of-charge. For NGV2-compliant station design, use SAE J2601 thermal modeling software.

Reference Standard: NGV2 / NFPA 52

NFPA 52 governs CNG vehicular fuel system design and station safety in the US. NGV2 defines the global standard for CNG cylinder filling protocols, including maximum fill rates and pressure-temperature compensation requirements.

CNG Pressure Cascade: What Does It Mean?

A CNG Pressure Cascade refers to a CNG filling system found in gas filling stations. It is used to dispense gas into the vehicle's tank at high pressure, generally at 200 bar (3000 psi) or 250 bar (3600 psi). A pressure differentiation method is used in the filling process.

How Does CNG Cascade Filling Work?

Instead of drawing gas from a single large storage vessel, gas is stored sequentially into three different pressure tanks—low, medium, and high-pressure tanks—and gas is drawn from each tank sequentially.

  • Low Pressure Banks: Initially, when a vehicle arrives at a gas filling station, gas is pushed into the vehicle's tank from this bank.
  • Medium Pressure Banks: After filling some gas at low pressure, when the vehicle's tank is moderately filled, gas is pushed from medium pressure banks.
  • High Pressure Banks: After pushing gas from the medium pressure bank, gas is pushed from high pressure banks to ensure the required 200 bar or 250 bar is reached.

This sequential filling ensures that the compressor does not have to run all the time and allows steady filling at a low energy cost. A priority panel automatically controls the switching based on pressure differentials.

Typical CNG Cascade Pressure Ranges

The exact pressure ranges depend on the filling station's design. Local regulations like NGV2 (ANSI/NGV 2) and ISO 11439 / ISO 15500 also play a critical role in the cascade pressure. However, a standard 200 bar gas filling cascade setup should follow:

  • Low Pressure Bank: 25 to 50 bar
  • Medium Pressure Bank: 50 to 120 bar
  • High Pressure Bank: 120 to 250 bar

Important Note

The High Pressure Tank must be maintained at a pressure at least 20% higher than the vehicle's tank for a complete fill.

What Factors Affect CNG Dispenser Pressure?

Several factors affect the CNG Dispenser Pressure. The four primary factors are:

  • Initial Vehicle Tank Pressure: A nearly empty vehicle tank allows for a higher initial mass flow rate (kg/min) due to the greater pressure differential (ΔP) between the bank and the vehicle.
  • Cascade Bank Volumes: The physical volume of the cascade banks (low, medium, and high) affects the flow.
  • Ambient and Gas Temperature: Temperature affects gas density and pressure.
  • Flow Rate: Faster filling produces heat and temporarily spikes pressure. This leads to an underfill once the gas cools.

CNG Cascade Pressure vs. Dispenser Pressure

It is important to understand the difference between CNG Cascade Pressure and Dispenser Pressure. Cascade Pressure means the pressure in the storage tanks, and Dispenser Pressure refers to the pressure at the nozzle before the gas enters the vehicle tank. Cascade Pressure is static, and Dispenser Pressure fluctuates through hoses, valves, and meters. Dispenser Pressure always remains slightly lower than the Cascade Pressure.

CNG Cascade Pressure and Temperature

Temperature is the silent enemy in CNG filling. When CNG is filled rapidly at 200 bar into the vehicle tank, the temperature of the gas increases. This leads to a sudden increase in gas volume and results in an underfill when the ambient temperature of the gas cools down. Modern cascade systems and dispensers use temperature compensation (following NGV2 and ISO 11439 standards) to adjust the filling pressure dynamically based on the gas temperature.

Why CNG Cascade Pressure Matters in Fueling

Optimizing the cascade pressure is not just about physics; it is also about business and safety. Here are some important aspects that you must consider:

  • Customer Satisfaction: An unsatisfied customer means a loss in business. A customer does not want an underfilled tank, which they have paid for. Maintaining exact pressure and refill satisfies the customer and improves business capacity in the long run.
  • Operational Efficiency: It reduces the run time of the compressor and saves electricity and cost.
  • Safety: It prevents overpressure, which may trigger Pressure Relief Devices (PRDs) and vent gas into the atmosphere.
