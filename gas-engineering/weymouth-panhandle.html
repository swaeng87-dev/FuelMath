Methodology & Formula

The Weymouth and Panhandle equations are empirical models developed by the American Gas Association (AGA) for sizing and rating high-pressure natural gas transmission pipelines. All inputs use imperial units with base conditions of 60°F (520°R) and 14.7 psia.

Weymouth Equation

Q = 433.5 × (Tb/Pb) × √[(P₁² − P₂²) × D16/3 / (S × Tf × L × Z)]

Panhandle A

Q = 435.87 × E × (Tb/Pb)1.0788 × [(P₁² − P₂²) / (S0.8539 × Tf × L × Z)]0.5394 × D2.6182

Panhandle B

Q = 737 × E × (Tb/Pb)1.02 × [(P₁² − P₂²) / (S0.961 × Tf × L × Z)]0.51 × D2.53

  • Q = Flow rate (SCFD), E = Pipeline efficiency (default 0.92)
  • P₁, P₂ = Upstream/downstream pressures (psia), D = Inside diameter (in)
  • S = Gas specific gravity, Tf = Flow temp (°R), L = Length (mi), Z = Compressibility

Reference Standard: AGA Gas Transmission

Weymouth is best for short, small-diameter lines. Panhandle A suits moderate-diameter lines, while Panhandle B is preferred for large-diameter, high-pressure transmission mains. For custody-accurate results, pair this tool with the Z-Factor Calculator to derive a precise compressibility value.
