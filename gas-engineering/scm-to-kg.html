Methodology & Formula

This calculator determines the physical mass of natural gas based on its volumetric flow or storage size at standard reference conditions.

m = V × ρ
  • m = Mass (kg)
  • V = Volume at standard conditions (SCM)
  • ρ = Density of the gas mixture (kg/m³)

Reference Standard: ISO 13443

Standard reference conditions for natural gas custody transfer and volumetric measurement are defined as 15°C (288.15 K) and 101.325 kPa (1 atm). Ensure your SCM volume is corrected to these base conditions before calculating mass.

Cubic Meters (CM) and Standard Cubic Meter (SCM)

The volume of gas is generally measured in Cubic Meters (CM). But the word 'Standard' SCM refers to standard condition ie, 15°C (288.15 K) temperature and 1 atmosphere pressure (101.325 kPa). For calculating the quantity of PNG (Piped Natural Gas), SCM (Standard Cubic Meter) is used. The composition of PNG differs from distributor to distributor, and from region to region. The main component of PNG is Methane. But it also contains other hydrocarbons like Ethane, Propane, Butane, and incombustible gases like CO₂, N₂ and inert gases in negligible quantities. PNG is generally 90% to 95% Methane. As there is no fixed Methane ratio and it flows through pipes in different compositions, calculating the gas in SCM becomes difficult.

SCM to KG

But PNG is measured in SCM. So it becomes more difficult to convert this SCM into KG. Generally, 1 SCM of Piped Natural Gas is about 0.71 to 0.80 kg. The difference is due to the composition of the gas. Pure Methane is approximately 0.71 kg in standard conditions. As Methane is a light gas, the more Methane in the PNG, the lighter it is. For commercial PNG billing, you can consider 1 SCM of PNG is equivalent to 0.76 kg.

Why PNG in KG

I think people want PNG in KG terms for two reasons. The first is purely for academic purposes. For several gas engineering calculations, a student needs the result in kg.

The second reason is that people have been used to CNG and LPG for a long time. Both CNG and LPG are measured in KG. But when people shifted to PNG, they saw their consumption bill in SCM. They got confused. They are not familiar with SCM. So, for better understanding of how much PNG they are consuming and how much they are saving in gas bill, they want the result in KG. They want to check if they are benefiting after shifted to PNG or not? So they try to understand their gas consumption quantity by converting their SCM bill to KG terms.

SCM to Metric Ton

If you wish to get the result in Metric Tons (MT), you should divide the kg value by 1000. Metric Tons = kg ÷ 1000. For 1 SCM of PNG, assuming 0.76 kg, divide it by 1000, and you will get 0.00076 MT. {(0.76 ÷ 1000) = 0.00076}. Let's check the table below for quick reference.

Difference between SCM, Sm³ and Nm³

SCM and Sm³ are same. 'S' stand for 'Standard', and 'm³' is the abbreviation for cubic meter (CM). It refers to gas volume at 15°C (288.15 K). But Nm³ is different. It stands for Normal Cubic Meter. It refers to gas volume at 0°C (273.15 K) and 1 atmosphere (101.325 kPa) of pressure.

Gas at 0°C is denser than the gas at 15°C. For a rough conversion:

1 Nm³ ≈ 1.06 Sm³ (at 15°C)
